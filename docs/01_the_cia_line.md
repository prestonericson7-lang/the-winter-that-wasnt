# 1. The CIA line, record by record (1960 to 1984)

Every record below was read on the CIA's own reading room ([cia.gov/readingroom](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/)) on November 22, 2026. Dates are the publication dates on each record's own page; where a bullet's date comes from the record's own text instead (a memo's own date, the day of a meeting), the page's date is given beside it. Quotes are the reading room's own text of the scans (OCR), with words broken across lines
joined and plain one-word misreadings put right (the OCR's "84$" is quoted as "84%", its "usir" as "using"); letters the OCR lost are
restored in brackets; where the scan cuts off, it says so. Names blacked out in the records are marked [sanitized].

## Before the perceptron

- **October 5, 1960.** Less than two months after the first successful CORONA satellite mission (August 18, 1960), and before the National
  Photographic Interpretation Center existed (NPIC was created in January 1961), Arthur Lundahl's memo to the CIA's deputy director for
  intelligence sets the goals, as Jack O'Connor quotes it: "To develop instrumentation which will demonstrate the feasibility of machines
  distinguishing between man-made and natural features on photography," and "The ultimate development of equipment for locating specific
  shapes on photography as a means of target recognition." Lundahl "reallocated some of his 1961 operating budget to fund an initial target
  recognition initiative" ([O'Connor, 2023](../SOURCES.md#oconnor)).
- **September 1961, in public.** Albert E. Murray of Cornell Aeronautical Laboratory publishes "Perceptron Applications in Photo
  Interpretation" in *Photogrammetric Engineering*, under Office of Naval Research contract Nonr-3161(00) (UNVERIFIED: from a search engine's
  reading of the paper; see [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md#murray)).
- **1962, in public.** Malcolm R. Uffelman of SCOPE Incorporated, Falls Church, Virginia, publishes "CONFLEX I, A Conditioned Reflex System" in
  the IRE International Convention Record: a learning machine whose "D-cells are majority decision gates" (read in the original, see
  [docs/02](02_the_contractor.md)).

## The second learning machine: CONFLEX (1964 to 1965)

- **January 24, 1964:** a contractor letter on identifying photographic imagery in two operations, normalization and "the adaptive pattern-
  recognition operation which is CONFLEX," adding that "all pertinent documents and reports relating to the image-processing system and the
  CONFLEX are in the possession of the customer" ([CIA-RDP78B04770A002100010001-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002100010001-8)).
- **August 1964 to June 1965:** eleven monthly narrative reports on a "prenormalizer" whose measurements "have minimal change with translation
  and rotation," with testing "to be accomplished on the CONFLEX I Adaptive Recognition System" (first report, August 15, 1964,
  [CIA-RDP78B04770A002100010029-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002100010029-8)).
- **June 15, 1965, the results** (eleventh report, [CIA-RDP78B04770A002100010011-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002100010011-7)):
  "six examples of simulated railyard imagery were trained individually in six classes on the CONFLEX System. Each of the six was taught in a
  standard position," then tested at nine rotations up to 45 degrees: "the system response was correct in all but three of the 54 test
  positions." Airport runways against housing developments: "Five slides of each class were used to program the CONFLEX System and all
  thirty-four slides were then tested in the recognition mode. No errors in classification were made for any of the thirty-four slides which
  were tested in each of four orientations at 90 degree intervals." The same report: "the failure of the CONFLEX Memory System."
- **A Soviet report on it.** The same NPIC file keeps a translation titled "A Soviet report on the American 'CONFLEX' system," from an
  engineer-colonel's article in the Moscow newspaper *Sovetskiy Patriot*, 16 August (the year is unreadable in the scan)
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A002100010031-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002100010031-5)). The machine the CIA was
  testing had been written up in the Soviet military press.

## After the perceptron: "an adaptive, automatic, target recognition system" (1965 to 1967)

- **March 19, 1965:** proposal TF-288, "Development of an Automatic Target Recognition System"
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A000200020023-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a000200020023-4)); the 50-page technical proposal
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A000200020022-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a000200020022-5)): "Limited forms of image
  processing and subsequent adaptive recognition processes have, to this point, produced encouraging results." The contractor [sanitized] built
  "CONFLEX I, a large capacity, conditioned-reflex system," "a general-purpose pattern-recognition system ... built nearly three years ago," whose
  threshold cells are wired at random: "a pseudo-random number-generator sets switches in a cross-bar matrix to which the input signals are
  attached." It had already tested CONFLEX on "30 sets of prenormalized data that represented TITAN site imagery": "Correct identifications were
  made of all the training examples presented to the CONFLEX system and of 87 percent of" (the scan cuts off there).
- **March 22, 1965 (record dated April 1):** the R&D catalog form
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A000200020020-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a000200020020-7)). The aim: "the development of an
  adaptive, automatic, target recognition system. It will be designed so as to aid operational interpreters by relieving them of the time
  consuming task of locating and classifying targets of interest in large volumes of intelligence imagery." The reason: "The rate of acquisition
  of reconnaissance photography is increasing more rapidly than can be accommodated by a simple increase in exploitation manpower." The
  perceptron's flaw, in the agency's own words: "before a computer of the Perceptron type could be taught to recognize an unknown image as
  belonging to a class, the unknown image had to be in the same orientation and in the same part of the field as the images which were used
  previously in training the machine. If the unknown images were re-oriented or translated, the computer probably would not recognize them."
  The contractor: Falls Church, Virginia, July 1965 to March 1966. And: "Many agencies are working in this field."
- **June 30, 1965:** the negotiated contract; inspection reports from September 1965 to July 1966 (in
  [data/automatic_target_recognition_records.csv](../data/automatic_target_recognition_records.csv)).
- **October 31, 1965: General Motors, by name.** A consultant's status report to NPIC (this copy went out with cover letters dated November 30, the date on its page; the second copy, below, is dated October 31), under "Automatic Target
  Recognition": "Over the past few months the General Motors Defense Research Laboratory at Santa Barbara, California, has done further work on
  the transformation of aerial photo imagery by generating the fraunhofer diffraction pattern with coherent light from a laser. They were
  encouraged by the distinctiveness of the signatures of various terrain features and culture. I suggest a visit by the technical staff to GMDRL
  in the near future is in order to examine the recent work"
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A002900010011-9](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002900010011-9)). The same paragraph survives in
  a second copy, filed as "Invoice #4" ([CIA-RDP78B04770A002900010021-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002900010021-8)),
  where the reviewers blacked out the name ("Over the past few months the [sanitized] has done further work on the transformation of aerial
  photo imagery") but left "GMDRL" in the last sentence. The laboratory's name was meant to stay hidden; the black bar missed it twice.
- **January 26, 1966:** "Automatic Target Recognition Program"
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A001700010012-1](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a001700010012-1)). O'Connor reads this as a new
  contract, through 1967, "NPIC's first research effort at automatic target recognition that excluded the Perceptron," and the record's
  justification is that the Army's Tactical Target Recognizer, GIMRADA's Natural Image Computer, Rome Air Development Center's Automatic
  Target Recognition Device and Stanford's MINOS II "all have serious deficiencies when related to the ultimate operational requirements."
- **April 29, 1966:** "Final Technical Report: Analysis of Prenormalization Techniques for Imagery Screening," 76 pages
  ([CIA-RDP78B04770A000200020017-1](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a000200020017-1)). Its block diagram: film, optical
  scanner, video signal, video processor, "ADAPTIVE RECOGNITION SYSTEM," classifications. Its figures include an "Adaptive Learning Program" on a
  G-15 computer and another on an SDS-925, "Random Orientation of Imagery," "Standardized Orientation of Imagery," and "Correlation of Filter
  Outputs for Tanks-Versus-Background Experiments." **The perceptron left NPIC by name. A learning machine of the same family, random-wired
  threshold cells trained on examples, stayed one more year.**
- **June 3, 1966, the honest end:** the contract inspection report ([CIA-RDP78B04770A002100010006-3](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a002100010006-3)):
  on "failure of the Conflex memory units" the contractor "was forced to improvise another recognition program on a general purpose computer,"
  and "the system ... correctly recognized 60% of targets on actual aerial photography under a wide range of translational and rotational
  conditions."
- **1967:** the Mark I Perceptron goes to the Smithsonian under a transfer administered by the Office of Naval Research (O'Connor).
  "Automatic Target Recognition (ATR) Task No. 6: NPIC Operations," 39 pages, January 1, 1967
  ([CIA-RDP99T01396R000300440001-2](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp99t01396r000300440001-2)).

## Inside the public winter (1969 to 1974)

- **February to April 1969:** the ATR program's feasibility demonstration of automatic cloud screening, using "the electronic clue extraction
  technique and diffraction pattern sampling technique ... and possibly the matched filtering approach" (R&D status report, February 7, 1969,
  [CIA-RDP78B05171A000600020184-9](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000600020184-9)).
- **June 9, 1969: "faster machine learning algorithms."** The Phase IV contract, dated May 12, 1969, for sixteen months: about 80% of the
  money for an engineering prototype Automatic Target Indexing Device (ATID), and "The re[mainder] of the funding will be used for sustaining
  research and development of related ATR techniques for real-time filter generation, change detection and faster machine learning
  algorithms" (R&D status report, [CIA-RDP78B05171A000800070050-0](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000800070050-0);
  a twin copy is [CIA-RDP78B05171A000600020127-2](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000600020127-2)). Agency money for
  machine learning, in those words, in the year the public winter began.
- **June 20, 1969: an "adaptive learning network."** An NPIC officer's report on visits in California in May 1969, "Development of an
  Image-Processing Rationale" (SECRET, [CIA-RDP78B05171A000800070025-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000800070025-8)),
  describes a sanitized company's coherent-optical diffraction system, "designed and developed ... starting in 1961" and built "without
  external funding," whose first objective is "to develop a rapid automated signature analysis capability with an adaptive learning network
  dependent, in part, on human intuition and recognition abilities"; on an IBM 360-65, "the computer will recognize the area (e.g., natural,
  man-made" ... in "less than 10 seconds." Who it was is a [shot in the dark](06_shots_in_the_dark.md).
- **June 1969, by name:** a progress report on the device's interfaces schedules meetings "for the veek [week] of 16-20 June with representatives of PSD/Litton" ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000800070052-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000800070052-8), read in the Internet Archive's mirror): the contractor's name, left in.
- **July 2 and September 2, 1969:** a technique evaluation team for the ATID weighs "a. Optical (diffraction pattern sampling)" against "b.
  Electronic (electronic clue extraction)" ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000800070008-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000800070008-7));
  on September 2 NPIC decides to "Proceed with the fabrication of an engineering prototype target indexing device (target look-through) using
  the diffraction pattern sampling (optical) technique," and asks for "a cost and work statement for a demonstratable electronic breadboard as a parallel back-up effort for the optical prototype" (read on the page image: the reading room's OCR drops "a demons-" and reads "tratable," which an earlier draft of this page wrongly offered as "trainable") (RED monthly report,
  September 8, 1969, [CIA-RDP78B04747A000300020039-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04747a000300020039-5)).
- **September 1969: the money was going up.** The CIA's annual report to the President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board (TOP SECRET,
  [CIA-RDP75B00159R000100060026-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp75b00159r000100060026-7)), on processing the flood of
  collected material: "The importance attached to this problem by Agency intelligence production managers is reflected in the substantial
  increases in resources allocated during the past few years. The trend is expected to continue for several years to come. Specific projects
  include: (a) development of automatic target recognition techniques."
- **October 2, 1969:** "NPIC R&D Program FY 1971" lists "Auto Target Pattern Recognition"
  ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000200030002-3](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000200030002-3)).
- **October 20, 1969 (SECRET):** "Special Purpose Computers": "The four program areas utilizing the computers as an integral part of the project
  are: 1) High Precision Stereo Comparator; 2) Automatic Stereo Scanner; 3) Automatic Target Recognition; and 4) the ORD image restoration
  equipment." Automatic Target Recognition gets a Honeywell DDP-516 minicomputer, with "A second DDP-516 system" also part of the program
  ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000100010172-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000100010172-8)). Ordinary hardware of the day.
- **December 1, 1969:** a study of what a near-real-time collection system would mean for the CIA's imagery analysts: "Some photointerpretation functions could perhaps be done by machine (e.g., a cloud scanner, or a target recognition device), although probably at great expense. But beyond these are other functions which cannot be automated" ([CIA-RDP78B05703A000700060016-6](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05703a000700060016-6), read in the Internet Archive's mirror).
- **March 1970:** NPIC's five-year plan for FY-72 to FY-76: "new programming techniques will provide AID with the capability to serve pattern
  recognition, digital image manipulation, etc."
  ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000500010011-2](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000500010011-2)).
- **July 22, 1970: at Litton.** A TOP SECRET cable of July 13, 1970 sends an officer to "AN INFORMAL MEETING ON JULY 22, 197Ø AT LITTON VAN NUYS, CALIF. FOR REVIEW OF TARGET INDEXING DEVICE (CLOUD SCREENER)" ([CIA-RDP78B03824A000600010059-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b03824a000600010059-7), read on the page image; the OCR reads the slashed zero as a 6, which would put the meeting in 1976).
- **November 12, 1970:** the test and evaluation of the "Target Indexing Device (TID)," "an engineering prototype," "the result of the Phase IV
  task of the Automatic Target Recognition Program," meant "to provide an automatic cloud screening technique for use in the target indexing
  operation normally performed (manually) during initial handling of newly acquired imagery prior to interpretation," designed to scan film "at
  a speed of 100 feet per minu[te]" ([CIA-RDP78B05703A000100030009-3](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05703a000100030009-3)).
  Four years of target recognition research came out as a machine for telling clouds from ground.
- **December 1 and 2, 1970: the CIA in the room.** NPIC's report on that year's "Symposium on Automatic Photointerpretation and Recognition" (TEG/OD/TPB-164/70, December 14, 1970, [CIA-RDP80T01137A000200040001-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp80t01137a000200040001-4), read in the Internet Archive's mirror): "the latest in what has become a series of annual symposia on automatic photointerpretation and recognition," at the Westinghouse Defense and Space Center in Baltimore, "some 125 persons," sponsored by the Electronic Industries Association, "an outgrowth of a series of automatic target recognition contract review meetings." "NPIC and CIA were well represented," and the chairman of its subcommittee on symposia, "an ostensible U.S. Army civilian employee, is actually a DDS & T/ORD officer": a CIA research officer under cover, running the open community's meetings (the memo blacks out his name, and so does this page). On the program, "A learning system for terrain recognition" from Bendix, which the NPIC reviewer wrote up as an "adaptive band-pass filter ... capable of changing the frequency up or down in response to 'training,'" "84% accurate as measured against a human control" but "not appropriate to finding objects." His verdict on the meeting: "Decidedly in the minority were papers concerning pattern recognition systems or hardware in actual use or contemplated for early development."
- **December 1970: the device arrives.** "Automatic Target Indexing Device - This equipment was delivered to NPIC in December and installed in Room 2N617. It is currently being "debugged" and should be available for demonstrations and T&E by mid-January" (RED monthly report, TCS-10311/71, January 11, 1971, [CIA-RDP78B04747A000300020023-2](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04747a000300020023-2)); a memo on the contract's overrun allows "the cost of maintaining Litton personnel, at NPIC for one week for training personnel in the operation of the TID" ([CIA-RDP78B05703A000200030013-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05703a000200030013-7)). Who built it, and where its technique went next: [docs/02](02_the_contractor.md#a-third-name-the-devices-maker).
- **February 1971 (record dated May 10):** the FY-72 R&D planning conference
  ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000200010004-3](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000200010004-3)): "A study to determine if current
  technology of Automatic Target Recognition could be applied as an aid to the interpretation of radar imagery"; an "Automated Target Rescan
  Device" to "Investigate the possibility of developing a system which can memorize the information at a target site and then recognize this
  and compare and identify it on other imagery"; and diffraction pattern sampling to detect "new activity in remote areas."
- **April 7, 1971:** the program list asks "if the current technology in Diffraction Pattern Sampling can be extended to the detection of new
  activity in remote areas," beside a "SLAR/ATR Feasibility Study" for radar imagery
  ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000200010002-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000200010002-5)).
- **August 1972:** an NPIC monthly report on a visit where staff saw "a significant advancement" in real-time optical filter and hologram
  generation, and "their small in-house effort in pattern recognition"
  ([CIA-RDP78B04747A000300020005-2](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04747a000300020005-2)).
- **September and December 1972: the device goes to the community.** The Committee on Imagery Requirements and Exploitation (COMIREX) puts
  "Target Indexing Device Performance Analysis (45 minutes)" on its agenda for September 7, 1972
  ([CIA-RDP79B01709A000100030111-6](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp79b01709a000100030111-6)) and "Prototype Target Indexing
  Device (TID) (COMIREX-D-30.5/1, 8 December 1972)" on a later one
  ([CIA-RDP79B01709A000100030104-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp79b01709a000100030104-4)); NPIC's September 1972 monthly
  report says development objectives "for operation and maintenance manuals for the Image Translator System and the Target Indexing Device
  were completed" ([CIA-RDP78B04747A000300020004-3](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04747a000300020004-3); its page's date, January 1, 1972, is a placeholder).
- **1973 to 1974, a strange one:** a CIA contract to study how weakly electric fish recognize patterns: "the relationship between
  electroreceptors in the pattern recognition process of electric fish would be studied," then "physical analogs of the electroreceptors of
  electric fish to be used in subsequent studies of underwater object detection, identific[ation]" (the scan cuts off)
  ([document 00173502](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/00173502)). Learning from a living pattern recognizer, inside the winter.
- **December 5, 1974:** an intelligence-community R&D subcommittee lists its problem areas
  ([CIA-RDP80M01082A000300010001-1](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp80m01082a000300010001-1)), among them "Automated pattern
  recognition and change detection for imagery processing." **A thing it wanted, not a thing it had.**

## Ten years later

- **1976 to 1983: the quiet, by another name.** A March 1982 briefing by the CIA's Office of Research and Development lists its "PREVIOUS/ONGOING ORD RESEARCH EFFORTS": "INTELLIGENCE APPLICATIONS STUDIES (AMRD) - BBN (1976), SRI (1980)" and "IMAGE UNDERSTANDING (IERD) - HUGHES (1982)" (CONFIDENTIAL, [CIA-RDP95-00972R000100020004-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp95-00972r000100020004-5), read on the page image). Its new Intelligent Systems Program (February 4, 1982, [CIA-RDP95-00972R000100020003-6](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp95-00972r000100020003-6)) names "image understanding" among the Agency's applications; the community's recommendations on artificial intelligence (December 29, 1982, [CIA-RDP85B01152R000700910072-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp85b01152r000700910072-4); February 1, 1983, [CIA-RDP85M00364R000500770006-0](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp85m00364r000500770006-0)) name "expert systems, natural language processing, image understanding, and logic programming," and no learning machine; and on January 26, 1983 the Procurement Division received "A request to contract for an image understanding/artificial intelligence testbed program," to be "procured over a three year period at a cost of $1.7 million" ([CIA-RDP87-00352R000100010009-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp87-00352r000100010009-8)). All read in the Internet Archive's mirror.

- **May 10, 1984:** the intelligence community's Artificial Intelligence Steering Group trip report
  ([CIA-RDP86M00886R000500050006-9](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp86m00886r000500050006-9)) sorts image technologies into
  "USED TODAY" (image enhancement) and "NOT USED TODAY": "IMAGE PROCESSING - PATTERN RECOGNITION (FEATURE EXTRACTION)," "COMPUTER VISION,"
  "ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SCENE INTERPRETATION." Under the heading "ADAPTIVE TRAINING": "EACH TOOL HAS SEVERAL GAIN/DECISION/CONTROL
  PARAMETERS ... DIFFICULT TO TUNE BY HAND FOR A GIVEN CLASS OF IMAGERY," answered with ground truth, error analysis and an optimizer:
  learning, in the plainest sense, on the open world's tools (the Sobel edge operator, Maryland and Illinois software).

- **June 18, 1984:** the steering group's meeting minutes
  ([CIA-RDP86M00886R000500040011-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp86m00886r000500040011-4)): the Navy's AI center (NCARAI)
  has an ISAR ship classifier that "attempts to combine AI with existing, more traditional statistical pattern recognition techniques" and "In
  a recent test the system identified 84% of the targets correctly"; among "CRITICAL AI TECHNOLOGIES": "ADAPTIVE LEARNING SYSTEMS."

- **November 28, 1984: learning, in signals.** A CIA security committee file keeps a contractors' seminar roster
  ([CIA-RDP87T00623R000100080011-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp87t00623r000100080011-7)). One biography, of the manager of
  ESL, Inc.'s artificial intelligence center: before that post he "was responsible for automated SIGINT signal acquisition and classification
  technology at ESL," where "Concepts of machine learning and articifial intelligence were applied to the automatic classifiers to yield
  adaptive classification algorithms capable of improving performance during system operation." Signals, not imagery; the customer is not
  named, and signals intelligence has only government customers.
