# 2. The name under the black bar

The CIA's 1965 to 1966 automatic target recognition study was done by a contractor whose name is blacked out ([sanitized], "25X1") in every
released record. Two primary documents, one secret and one public, name the same machine, and that is enough to name the company.

## What the CIA records say about the contractor

- **Where:** "Falls Church, Virginia"; class of contractor: "Manufacturer"; July 1965 to March 1966 (R&D catalog form, March 22, 1965,
  [CIA-RDP78B04770A000200020020-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a000200020020-7)).
- **What it had built:** "[sanitized] has carried out studies on pattern recognition and wideband video-signal recognition under the following
  contracts:" (the list is sanitized) "CONFLEX I, a large capacity, conditioned-reflex system, was constructed under the second of these
  contracts," and "The CONFLEX I, a general-purpose pattern-recognition system, was built
  nearly three years ago," that is, about 1962. Its cells: "combined in various ways in summing circuits and clipped to simple binary or ternary
  values by threshold circuits (D-cells)," with "the sequence of processing used during learning"; and in CONFLEX "the combinations of inputs to the
  D-cell are selected arbitrarily; a pseudo-random number-generator sets switches in a cross-bar matrix to which the input signals are attached."
  It had also studied "the line integral scan (formerly termed 'conical transform')" under Air Force contracts (technical proposal TF-288,
  [CIA-RDP78B04770A000200020022-5](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b04770a000200020022-5)).

## What the open record says about CONFLEX I

The 1962 IRE International Convention Record, Part 4 (read in the original scan, [worldradiohistory.com](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-IRE/Convention-Record/IRE-1962-Convention-Record-Part-4-270125.pdf)),
page 132 by its table of contents: "CONFLEX I -- A CONDITIONED REFLEX SYSTEM -- Malcolm R. Uffelman, SCOPE Incorporated, Falls Church,
Virginia." Its summary: "The
functional organization of a conditioned-reflex system (CR System) is presented. This general system is then analyzed by means of probability
theory and design equations are derived. Finally the logical design and operational capabilities of a special purpose digital system, called
CONFLEX I, which implements the concept is described." And: "The D-cells are majority decision gates."

## The match

| | The CIA's [sanitized] contractor, 1965 | SCOPE Incorporated, 1962 |
|---|---|---|
| Town | Falls Church, Virginia | Falls Church, Virginia |
| Machine | CONFLEX I, "a large capacity, conditioned-reflex system" | CONFLEX I, "A Conditioned Reflex System" |
| Cells | threshold "D-cells," a "D-field" | "Discrimination Cells (D-Cells)," "majority decision gates" |
| Built | "nearly three years ago" (about 1962) | published 1962 |

**The verdict (ESTIMATE, near certain; the 1964 to 1966 records and the AQUILINE chart only strengthen it):** the contractor was SCOPE Incorporated of Falls Church, Virginia, and its engineer Malcolm R. Uffelman
built the learning machine the CIA's target recognition study grew from. No other company is known to have built a CONFLEX.

## What the same contractor did for NPIC before and after, in the released records

- **1964 to 1965:** a contract on a "prenormalizer" tested "on the CONFLEX I Adaptive Recognition System," with a January 1964 letter on "the
  integral scan sequence" (the line integral scan of TF-288) and eleven monthly narrative reports; in June 1965 a trained CONFLEX was right on
  51 of 54 rotated railyard tests and on all 136 runway-or-housing tests (34 slides in four orientations each). See
  [docs/01](01_the_cia_line.md#the-second-learning-machine-conflex-1964-to-1965).
- **1966:** after "failure of the Conflex memory units," a recognition program improvised on a general purpose computer: 60% of targets on
  actual aerial photography (contract inspection report, June 3, 1966).
- **1967 to 1968, by name:** a Project AQUILINE chart (the CIA's small reconnaissance drone; document
  [03055187](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/03055187), dated August 29, 1967, released 2020) lists "Scope, Inc." among its FY'68
  contractors. Unlike the target recognition records, this one was not sanitized.
- **1984:** Malcolm R. Uffelman is in the name lists of a newsletter of the Security Affairs Support Association, the intelligence
  contractors' association ([CIA-RDP87T00623R000100080014-4](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp87t00623r000100080014-4)), and
  of an April 20, 1984 letter from the Deputy Director of Central Intelligence, John N. McMahon, to William O. Baker
  ([CIA-RDP86M00886R002600030022-0](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp86m00886r002600030022-0)).

## The same company, in the open, before and after (UNVERIFIED: from a search engine's reading of each page; the pages were not opened here)

- "Target Detection, Prenormalization and Learning Machines," M. R. Uffelman, Scope Inc., Falls Church, Va., in the *Transactions of the
  Symposium on Photo Interpretation*: the CIA study's own subject, published under the company's name.
- SOCRATES, described as Scope Inc.'s adaptive learning machine, its name spelled out as Scope's own conditioned-reflex, automatic trainable
  electronic system.
- A March 31, 1967 final report by SCOPE Incorporated for the Office of Naval Research on information theory and learning systems, naming
  CONFLEX I, MINOS II and the Mark I Perceptron as adaptive systems (DTIC AD0656082; read in the Internet Archive's copy on the morning of November 22, so this one is no longer UNVERIFIED).
- Malcolm R. Uffelman and Warren L. Holford, "Time Domain Prenormalizer" ([US 3,566080](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/3566080), filed April 27, 1967, granted February 23, 1971, assigned to Scope Incorporated, Falls Church; read in the USPTO's copy): "An image-analyzing system using a rotating sweeping line scan to provide an input to a set of normalizing time domain filters which produce a set of outputs which describe the image in a manner independent of translation or rotation of the image." A prenormalizer of the kind the CIA's 1964 to 1965 contract tested on CONFLEX I (ESTIMATE), in the open.
- Malcolm R. Uffelman, inventor, Scope Inc.: a radar target pattern recognizer patented about 1980. If the record holds, the same engineer
  was still building target recognizers fifteen years after the CIA study, and the CIA's own 1971 plan had asked whether target recognition
  "could be applied as an aid to the interpretation of radar imagery."

## A second name under a black bar

The same release did it again with General Motors. A consultant's paragraph of October 31, 1965 on "Automatic Target Recognition" exists in
two copies: in one the laboratory is sanitized ("Over the past few months the [sanitized] has done further work on the transformation of aerial
photo imagery by generating the fraunhofer diffraction pattern"), and the last sentence still says "GMDRL"; in the other the whole name stands:
"the General Motors Defense Research Laboratory at Santa Barbara, California" ([docs/01](01_the_cia_line.md)). What the reviewers hid in
one place they left in another, which is how the 1969 records' sanitized diffraction company can be read too
([shot 2](06_shots_in_the_dark.md#2-the-company-that-taught-itself)).

## A third name: the device's maker

The contractor that built NPIC's target indexing device is sanitized in its contract papers and named three times in the same release ([docs/01](01_the_cia_line.md)): "representatives of PSD/Litton" at the device's interface meetings in June 1969 ([CIA-RDP78B05171A000800070052-8](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05171a000800070052-8)); a TOP SECRET cable of July 13, 1970 sending an officer to "AN INFORMAL MEETING ON JULY 22, 197Ø AT LITTON VAN NUYS, CALIF. FOR REVIEW OF TARGET INDEXING DEVICE (CLOUD SCREENER)" ([CIA-RDP78B03824A000600010059-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b03824a000600010059-7), read on the page image; the OCR turns the slashed zero into a 6 and the year into 1976); and a memo on the contract's overrun allowing "the cost of maintaining Litton personnel, at NPIC for one week for training personnel in the operation of the TID" ([CIA-RDP78B05703A000200030013-7](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp78b05703a000200030013-7)). ESTIMATE, strong: Litton Systems built the device. GM's Santa Barbara laboratory stays the strong estimate for the self-funded company of the May 1969 visit ([shot 2](06_shots_in_the_dark.md#2-the-company-that-taught-itself)); Litton was the contractor that built the machine.

Litton's people then put the technique in the open. Litton's "Photodetector Light Pattern Detector" ([US 3,689772](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/3689772), filed August 18, 1971, granted September 5, 1972; Nicholas George, James T. Thomasson and Abraham Spindel) samples a diffraction pattern with "eight concentric annular hemi-ring or zone detectors" and "34 radial sector detectors," for film in "a typical film transport," and points to Litton's "diffraction pattern sampling systems" application of September 20, 1971 by Jensen and others. Jensen, Thomasson and Harvey L. Kasdan then patented ring-and-wedge identification of fingerprints for Recognition Systems, Inc., also of Van Nuys ([US 3,944978](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/3944978), filed September 9, 1974). Thomasson had presented "Multiplexed holographic filters" for Litton at the December 1970 symposium NPIC attended. All read in the patents themselves, from the USPTO's own copies, and in the symposium report.

## What would settle it outright

The uncensored TF-288 contract, by a FOIA request to the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, which reviewed these records; or the Scope
Inc. report that went with the 1966 "Final Technical Report: Analysis of Prenormalization Techniques for Imagery Screening."
