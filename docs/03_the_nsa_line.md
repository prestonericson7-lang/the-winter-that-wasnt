## What it says (ESTIMATE)

- **The NSA wrote about learning machines in July 1958,** the same month the New York Times carried the Navy's perceptron, and again in 1959.
- **It named the hard problem early.** "The Rotation Problem in Pattern Recognition" appeared in Winter 1962, three years before the CIA's own
  R&D form gave rotation and translation as the reason to move past the perceptron.
- **Then it moved to voice.** After 1965 the pattern-recognition titles stop and the voice titles begin: the status of the voice problem
  (1967), speaker identification by machine (Fall 1969, the first year of the public winter), "Can We Lick the Voice Problem?" (1972), Markov
  models for phonetic text (1973), automatic scanning of speech channels (1975). The NSA's recognition problem was the flood of intercepted
  voice, and its journal kept at it straight through the years the open neural-network money dried up.
- **The tools were statistical, not neural.** From 1976 to 1978 come keyword spotting in intercepted voice, nearest-neighbor reference
  patterns, cluster analysis and statistical pattern recognition: the open field's tools of the same years. No title in the index after 1959
  names a learning machine, a perceptron or a neural network.
- **The learning went statistical, and the NSA's own contractor published it.** The method for training hidden Markov models came out of
  the Institute for Defense Analyses in Princeton, whose communications research division was set up in 1959 at the NSA's request and works
  under contract to it: L. E. Baum, T. Petrie, G. Soules and N. Weiss, "A Maximization Technique Occurring in the Statistical Analysis of
  Probabilistic Functions of Markov Chains," *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 41 (1970), 164 to 171 (the citation checked against Crossref's record; one of its authors, T. Petrie, gave his address as "Institute for Defense Analyses, Von Neumann Hall, Princeton, New Jersey" on his 1967 paper on the same chains, *PNAS* 57, 580; that IDA's division was founded at the NSA's request is UNVERIFIED, from a search engine's reading of IDA's own pages). "Markov Models for Phonetic Text" is in the NSA's journal three years later.
  That method, published in the open, became the backbone of speech recognition for the next forty years.
- The keyword-spotting titles of 1976 and 1977 sit beside the NSA's later "Google for Voice" (about 2005 to 2006, reported by
  [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2015/05/05/nsa-speech-recognition-snowden-searchable-text/) from the Snowden archive): the agency was
  asking how to find words in intercepted voice about thirty years before it could do it at scale.
