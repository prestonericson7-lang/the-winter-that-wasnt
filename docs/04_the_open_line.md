# 4. The open line: what anyone could read in the same years

Set beside the agencies' records, the open literature shows where each idea stood in public at the time. Every row says how its source was read: in the original, in a report's own pages, on the publisher's page, or only against a catalog record. UNVERIFIED marks what was found only through a search engine's reading or a catalog listing and not opened here; the links are in [SOURCES](../SOURCES.md). The rows were re-checked from square one on the morning of November 22, 2026 (see the [research log](../research_log.md)).

## What it says (ESTIMATE)

- **In every pair I could line up, the open paper came first or at the same time.** Nothing in the released agency record is a learning-machine
  idea the open world did not already have.
- **The winter was a drought, not a stop.** Learning machines went on in the open under other names (adaptive decision networks, conditioned reflex systems, self-organizing networks, adaptive networks of goal-seeking components), and defense money reached them in every year from 1969 to 1985 that I could check: the Navy, the Pentagon's research agency through the Navy, and the Air Force (every year but 1973 read in a source; the list is in the [research log](../research_log.md) and beside P-W7 in [PREDICTIONS](../PREDICTIONS.md)).
- **The biggest gap was the one both sides could see:** recognizing a thing wherever it sits in the picture. The CIA wrote it down in 1965; the
  open world's answer was the neocognitron in 1980 and the convolutional networks that followed it.
