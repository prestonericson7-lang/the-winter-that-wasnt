# 5. Was the winter only public?

The shot this repository chased, from the earlier ai-roots research: "the AI winter was only public," meaning the agencies kept neural-network
work going through the years the public money dried up, perhaps far ahead of the open world. Here is what their own released records say
(ESTIMATE throughout, built on the MEASURED counts and quotes in docs 01 to 04).

## What holds

- **The learning did not stop in 1969.** In September 1969 the CIA told the President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board that
  resources for processing its collection had seen "substantial increases ... during the past few years," a trend "expected to continue for
  several years to come," with "development of automatic target recognition techniques" the first project named. NPIC's June 9, 1969 status report puts agency money into "faster machine learning algorithms" under a
  sixteen-month contract that ran into 1970; the same contract built a target indexing device that the intelligence community's imagery
  committee reviewed in September and December 1972. In May 1969 a company showed an NPIC officer "an adaptive learning network" for imagery, built on its own money. By 1984 a
  signals-intelligence contractor had machine learning inside its automatic signal classifiers.
- **In the open, the services kept the thread alive.** Read in the sources, a defense dollar reached neural or adaptive pattern recognition in every year from 1969 to 1985 but 1973: the Navy for Grossberg's learning networks from 1969, the Air Force for a trained imagery classifier (published 1971) and for its own scientist's theory of adaptive networks (1972), the Pentagon's research agency through the Navy (1974 to 1976), the Air Force's "Adaptive Network Simulations" from 1977, the work that became reinforcement learning, and the Navy for backpropagation in 1985 (docs/04).
- **It changed form.** The CIA's learning machines of 1963 to 1966 (the perceptron, then CONFLEX, random-wired threshold cells trained on
  examples) gave way to optical diffraction sampling and trained statistical decisions. The NSA's journal moved from learning machines (1958)
  to voice: speaker identification by machine (1969), Markov models for phonetic text (1973), keyword spotting, nearest neighbors and
  clustering (1976 to 1978). The method for training hidden Markov models came from the NSA's own contract mathematicians in Princeton (ESTIMATE, strong: one of its authors gave his address as the Institute for Defense Analyses in Princeton in 1967; see docs/03).
- **The perceptron left early, and by name only.** The word leaves the CIA's own records in 1965, four years before *Perceptrons*. The reason
  the agency's own form gives is rotation and translation, not the 1969 book.
- **The secret was the customer, not the science.** The perceptron work ran through a Navy contract and its reports were unclassified;
  CONFLEX was in a 1962 convention record and in the Soviet military press; the diffraction technique NPIC chose in 1969 was in the
  *Proceedings of the IEEE* in 1970; the hidden Markov method was in the *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* in 1970. And the agency sat inside the open community: at the industry's yearly symposium on automatic photo-interpretation in December 1970, the chairman of its subcommittee on symposia, listed as a U.S. Army civilian, was by NPIC's own memo "actually a DDS & T/ORD officer" (docs/01).

## What does not hold

- **No vault, no leap.** In every case I could line up, the open literature had the idea first or in the same year. The rotation problem was
  open in 1962 (Hu's moment invariants), three years before CONFLEX's prenormalizer beat it on railyards. And the agencies' own scorecards are
  modest: 60% of real targets in 1966; in December 1974 an intelligence-community R&D subcommittee still listed automated pattern recognition
  for imagery as a problem area; in May 1984 its AI steering group put pattern recognition and computer vision under "NOT USED TODAY," and a
  Navy radar classifier scored 84% in a prototype.
- **No neural network appears.** Not one released CIA record from 1969 to 1985 describes an agency neural network; the NSA's index names no
  learning machine after 1959.

## The one-line answer

The winter came inside the agencies too, but later and quieter, and it was a winter of names more than of learning: the money for machine
learning ran at least into 1970, the work moved from neural-style machines to optics and statistics, the best of it was published in the open
the same year, and by the agencies' own accounts in 1974 and 1984 the machines still could not do the job.

## What the released record cannot show

Everything still classified, and everything never put into CREST. After December 1972 the target indexing device leaves the record; the
reasons, and the other gaps, are in [shots in the dark](06_shots_in_the_dark.md).
